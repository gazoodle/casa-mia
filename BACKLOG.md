## Cameras, Camera Commander and the compositor

- **Cameras: every detection a camera has, and choosing the main camera by them** (asked 2026-10-09). Cameras today know one motion sensor each, and (b16) the camera's own motion detection switch. Cameras such as UniFi Protect's offer far more: binary sensors for a person, vehicle, animal, package, licence plate, face and object; sounds (baby cry, speaking, barking, glass break, siren, car alarm, car horn; smoke and CO alarms); and states such as is dark. Most come with their own detection switch on the camera's device ("Detections: Person" and so on), plus event entities (smart detection, sound, vehicle) that say what was seen and when.
  - **Gather them in discovery** (the Cameras module): for each camera, every detection sensor on its device, paired with the switch that enables it, by kind. Match by the integration's translation keys where it has them (UniFi Protect: `person_detected` with `detections_person`), else by device class and name; keep a small table of known kinds (label, icon) and show an unknown one by its own name. Generic, not one integration only.
  - **Show them on the Cameras page:** per camera, which detections it has and which are switched off (as b16 does for motion), and live in its preview: each detection's state, since when, and its switch.
  - **Later, choose the main camera by them** (the commander's Track motion, the "switch to" rules): per commander, which kinds count (a person or a vehicle, not a passing cat), perhaps a priority between them (a person over motion), and the hold, go back and pause times per kind. Is dark is a state, not an event: it could choose a camera's channel or the Security look rather than a camera.
  - **The card shows the kind,** not only a dot: the detection's own icon (a person, a car, a paw, a smoke alarm) in the tile's corner, pulsing as the dot does now, with the dot left for plain motion. The same options (size, colour, linger, corner), perhaps a colour per kind; the tooltip says what and when ("Person at 15:32:10").
  - **Open:** cameras with no smart detections (plain motion only, ESPHome tablets) keep today's behaviour; how to name and order the kinds when a camera sees several at once; whether alarms (smoke, CO, glass break) should raise more than a tile (a notification, the alarm panel).

- **Slow through Nabu Casa: cut the bandwidth** (first step done in 2026.10.3-b31: through HA the card caps its pixel ratio, `away_sharpness`, Balanced 1.5 by default; still to measure away, and the ideas below). Longer term, two ways to send only what changes: our own block updates (each stream keeps the last frame sent, sends only blocks that changed past a noise threshold, the card paints them on a canvas, a full frame now and then; big savings on quiet scenes, our own small codec), or real H.264 through go2rtc/WebRTC (HA ships go2rtc; best compression and hardware decoding, but an encoder per picture size and a `<video>` in the card). Automatic step-down on a slow link needs a signal the card lacks (an MJPEG `<img>` reports no frames in Safari; `navigator.connection` is Chrome only), so fixed levels first. Background: Away from home over Nabu Casa, with the compositor on, the whole HA UI was very slow; over a VPN to the LAN (IPv4, so the card takes the direct route) it was fast, so the path through HA and Nabu Casa is the problem, not the box. Measured 2026-10-06 with b30's figures (compositor `/status` `sending`, `pictures`, `round_s`): the device away held **two** streams at once through HA (one commander shown twice, 2208×1640 and 2208×1240 at dpr 2, 350–420 kB a frame), about **2.2 Mbit/s** together; a tablet at home took 280 kbit/s direct (1128×520). The box itself was busy but not choking: a round of about 30 stills took 1.65–2.6 s and drawing three pictures about 0.7 s, so a frame came every ~2.6 s rather than 2 s, the same at home and away; the compositor waited 0% to send (HA's proxy reads at LAN speed, so any wait is between HA and the viewer, logged by `pictures.py` at each stream's end: not yet seen). Suspects: the bytes (dpr 2 is four times the pixels of dpr 1), and HTTP/1.1's six connections per site (Nabu Casa passes the browser straight to HA's aiohttp, so each held stream takes one from the UI's own requests). Ideas: through HA, ask for dpr 1 (or a lower cap, or a lower JPEG quality) for about a quarter of the bytes; one stream per device through HA (end the older one rather than MAX_STREAMS = 3); a slower frame rate away; poll single frames (`.jpg`) instead of a held stream, which frees the connection between frames. First get the HA log's "picture … ended" lines (kbit/s, % waiting) from a session away (they are INFO, which HA hides by default: set the integration's log level to info first, e.g. `logger: logs: custom_components.casa_mia: info`), and compare UI responsiveness with each idea. Separately, the round could be shorter: small panel tiles need not be fetched every 2 s.

- **Commander text sizes.** The text a commander draws is fixed today: the camera names in each tile's bar, the main camera's name and time, "Changing to …", Stale and "no signal" (the compositor's `_fonts`: 16, 20 and 40 px, and the bar's `SMALL_BAR` height), only grown by the screen's density. They are layout options: into `layout.json` (labels, help, defaults), shown by the admin page and the card editors, and drawn by the compositor (the card's own overlays, such as its debug figures, can follow). A change redraws its pictures as any setting does (the reload after a save clears them).

E.g. a privacy switch, or a camera only worth showing while the gate is open. - **Hardware video decoding for the compositor's streams** (considered 2026-10-07, not started). - **Templates for card options.** Lovelace has no templating for card options, so build it once in `integration/cards/src/ha.ts`: any option may hold `{{ ... }}`, rendered through HA's `render_template` websocket subscription, which pushes a new value as soon as a state it reads changes (one subscription per templated option per card). ## Phone and SMS (FONA) and people

- **FONA Arduino firmware into the repo** (later; it works and needs no attention now). The sketch is `FonaForHA` (`FonaForHA.ino`, a FONA class, `InputBuffer.cpp/.h`, about 1,000 lines) on the Adafruit FONA library. It speaks the line protocol the `fona` module reads (`RING:`, `TEXT:`, `RSSI:`, `INFO: FONA <v> start|ready`, `OK`/`FAILED`; commands `START`, `ERASEALL`, `RSSI`, `SEND:`, `RESET`) and is the first PING layer ("PONG from Arduino", sent to any number before any allowlist check). Bring it in with a build (arduino-cli) and a build plan for the board, so others can make the same; this is now part of the public release. Changes worth making then (found while writing the app's `fona` module):
  - **Texts can be lost.** The sketch deletes each SMS from the SIM straight after printing it, with no acknowledgement, so a text that arrives while the app is restarting or the port is closed is gone. And `ERASEALL` on every start (asked for by the host, kept from the old integration) wipes anything still stored. Better: the host acknowledges each text (`ACK:<slot>`) before it is deleted, and on start the sketch reports stored texts instead of erasing them; then drop the host's `ERASEALL`.
  - **Replies don't match commands.** Only `SEND` and `ERASEALL` answer `OK`/`FAILED`; `START` answers `INFO ... ready` or `ERROR: FONA not found`, `RSSI` its own line, `RESET` and `AT...` nothing. One reply per command (or tagged replies) would let the host match them instead of special-casing each.
  - **Modem not found stays not found** until the host sends `START` again (the app retries every minute); the sketch could retry, or power-cycle the modem, itself.
  - **`RESET` jumps to address 0**, which does not reset the hardware; a watchdog reset does.
  - **Escaping:** new lines in texts go over serial as backslash-n, so a text that really contains a backslash followed by n arrives changed. Escape the backslash too.
  - **Long texts:** the read buffer is 250 characters and multi-part texts are not joined.
  - **Heartbeat:** the app probes a quiet line with `ALIVE?`; a periodic unsolicited line from the sketch would do the same with less traffic.

- **FONA voice calls into the voice assistant** (big, fun): instead of hanging up, answer an authorised call and connect the audio to HA's voice assistant (Assist pipeline: speech to text, a conversation agent, text to speech), so you can phone the house and talk to it; and let the house make calls (ring someone and speak a message, e.g. an alarm). Needs the FONA's audio (its mic/speaker pins, or a USB audio path) brought to the box, the Arduino sketch to answer and dial (`ATA`, `ATD`), and an audio bridge in the app. Research first: audio quality and latency over the FONA's analogue audio, and whether a different modem (USB with a UAC audio interface) is the better route.

- **People page: call and text simulator**. Next to **Check**, a **Test call** button and a **Test text** button (with a box for the message) that run the whole pipeline as if the Arduino had sent `RING:`/`TEXT:` for the number typed: PING handling, the People check, the `casa_mia_fona` event, the integration's event entities and PONG, and whatever HA automations do. Then report back what happened, if possible (the event fired, authorised or intrusion and why, which replies were attempted). **Must never send a real text**, or a test could spam a real person: while a simulation runs, the app absorbs every outgoing `SEND` caused by it (from its own PONG, the integration's PONG, and `casa_mia.send_sms` calls from automations) and logs it instead. Design points: mark simulated events (e.g. `simulated: true` in the event data) so the integration and automations can tell, and tie absorbed sends to the simulation (by number and a time window, or a simulation id carried through `send_sms`); a real text from the same number during a test must still go out. A simulated call that triggers the gate automation would really cycle the gates; decide whether automations should check `simulated` or whether that is the point.

- **FONA intrusion flood limit**: if one number sends many intrusions in a short time, fire fewer events (e.g. one a minute with a count). No floods seen so far.

- **Identity: one place for everything that identifies a person** (separate module, name to agree; builds on `people`). Today `people` holds only a name, one phone number, Call/Text permission and an optional HA person link, which is all FONA needs. This module adds the other pieces of data, from HA and elsewhere, that say who is at or near the house (occupants, guests, visitors, engineers), so they are managed in one place and every module can ask "who is this?":
  - **Vehicle registrations**, for the gate ANPR path. Today `Plate` entries in a to-do list, checked by a pyscript allowlist matcher with UK look-alike variants (`kind: plate`, e.g. 0/O, 1/I, 5/S). Port that matching; the todo list and the pyscript then retire.
  - **Device trackers**: the HA `device_tracker` entities (phones on the Wi-Fi, the companion app) that belong to each person.
  - **Guest login**: link endpoints and logins to people (who is staying, which suite, their car), and record what a visitor's phone tells us when it signs in (browser user agent and other browser variables), then drop a long-lived cookie once they are identified, so a returning phone is recognised.
  - Respect privacy: guests should be told what is kept (the welcome page can say so), and it should be easy to forget a person entirely.

## Guest login module (taking over `cnorick/ha-auto-guest-login`): complete, app 0.1.26

Kept as the design record; current behaviour is in the code and README.

Decision: it is part of this app's experience, so it goes in **our own app** as a Python module, not a separate app and not Node. Source read (MIT, Nathan Orick 2021, unmaintained since 2025-02, ~400 lines of Node/Express): `server.js`, `auth-client.js`, `ha-client.js`, `utils.js`, two EJS pages, DOCS.md.

What it does: a guest visits `http://<ha-host>:8675/`, the app logs the guest account in through HA's `/auth/login_flow` API, redirects the browser to a guest dashboard already authenticated, and fires the event `ha_auto_login_guest_logged_in` (data: `ip`). `/admin` (via ingress) shows the URL and a QR code. `?d=` / `?dashboard=` overrides the dashboard (done client-side in the welcome page).

**UI: rewrite, not port.** The upstream pages are clunky (fixed-size layout, rainbow-cycling title, a bare admin page). Proposals to refine when this starts: mobile-first welcome page in the app's look (guests are on phones), clear states for redirecting / failed login / HA unreachable, and an admin page with the link, a QR code and a printable QR card. Needs a design pass, so ask before building.

**Background (already in place and working, do not redesign):** guests get two QR codes. One joins the guest Wi-Fi; the other logs into HA and lands them on a locked-down dashboard at an unguessable URL. The rewrite only needs to keep that experience working with a nicer UI.

**Design (agreed; since built).** Two parts: an app module and a thin integration. The module name `guest-login` is final.

- **Endpoints, not one login.** Each QR code points at one endpoint, `http://<host>:8675/e/<slug>`: a label (a guest suite, a KNX panel), an unguessable random slug, an account, a landing dashboard, a type (guest page, or minimal engineer page) and an enabled flag. At least four guest suites, and QR codes for KNX panels and similar so visiting engineers can do maintenance and control by scanning. **The existing QR codes are immutable and must keep working exactly as they are**, so the legacy URL shapes (`/`, `?d=`) must be preserved alongside the new `/e/<slug>` ones. 
- **Existing codes (decoded from the printed QR cards):** all of the form `http://<box IP>:8675/?d=/guest-dashboards/<16 uppercase hex>`: the box's IP, port 8675, path `/`, and one random view path per suite inside a single `guest-dashboards` dashboard. One shared login today. Each becomes an endpoint carrying its `d` value as identity, so `/` with `?d=` resolves to it. The hex ids are the secret part; they are not recorded here. Behaviour changes, confirmed: an unknown `d` is refused (upstream redirected anywhere), and every endpoint starts off, so each must be switched on once (or by an automation) before the old guest login is retired (test one suite first). Printed cards need no reprint while the URL is unchanged.
- **Accounts per endpoint:** v1 points all legacy endpoints at the one default account (as today), but the model is one account per endpoint, so a suite can later move to its own HA user by changing the endpoint's account name; QR codes are unaffected. Separate users enable revoke-on-disable (switching an endpoint off also revokes that user's refresh tokens, logging out a guest already inside; impossible with a shared user, it would kick every suite), per-suite dashboard views via user conditions, and per-user logbook entries. HA has no per-user entity permissions, so this is not a hard lock-down.
- **Gating is the point.** Every endpoint is **off by default**; an automation (or you) turns it on, and the state is **remembered across app and HA restarts** (changed from the first design, which reset it on every restart and closed every suite on each update; saved in the app's data folder) (booking system, alarm state, a timed window). A disabled endpoint refuses the login with a plain "not available" page that gives nothing away. The unguessable URL and the switch are not a security boundary once someone is logged in, because an HA user is not confined to one dashboard; engineer accounts must be **non-admin** with a minimal dashboard. No PIN.
- **Accounts:** a default account plus extra named ones that endpoints refer to, in app options (passwords). The endpoint list (id, label, dashboard, type, account, slug, legacy) is the `guest_endpoints` app option too (decided: the legacy ids and slugs are secrets and stay out of git; the app options are validated by the schema and backed up with the app).
- **Guest pages:** welcome and login (signing in, success, failed, HA unreachable; mobile-first, the app's look), plus a house-info page (Wi-Fi, house rules; may be dropped later). Admin page (ingress): endpoints with their state, a QR per endpoint, a printable card, recent logins.
- **Integration:** a "Guest login" device with a child device per endpoint (found from the app's `/health`, so new endpoints appear on their own): an enable switch, last login, login count, and an event entity carrying the guest IP, for automations. Optional time limit on the switch. Not the legacy `ha_auto_login_guest_logged_in` event: nothing used it, the event entity replaces it.

Port notes and traps found:
- **Licence:** we are rewriting from scratch with a much nicer UI, using his code only as a description of the HA login flow. If none of his code or markup is carried over, no licence file or credit is needed. If a close port of any part survives, keep his MIT copyright and permission notice (`third_party/ha-auto-guest-login/LICENSE.md`). Use our own icon, not his logo (which is rawpixel's).
- **Environment:** the app's process does not get `SUPERVISOR_TOKEN` or image `ENV` under s6 (see the CM_COMPONENTS fix). Upstream used `#!/usr/bin/with-contenv bashio`. We need a `run.sh` with `#!/usr/bin/with-contenv bash` then `exec python3 -m casa_mia`.
- **Manifest:** `hassio_api: true`, `homeassistant_api: true`, `ports: 8675/tcp: 8675`, optional `guest_*` / `welcome_*` / `advanced_*` options (module disabled when username or password is empty, so the app still starts without them), ingress for `/admin`. Upstream's `armhf/armv7/i386`, `codenotary`, `image:` and `build.yaml` are all obsolete.
- **Supervisor calls:** HA port from `http://supervisor/core/info` (`data.port`), published guest port from `http://supervisor/addons/self/info` (`data.network['8675/tcp']`), event via `http://supervisor/core/api/events/<name>`.
- **Bug to fix in the port:** upstream builds the login JSON by string interpolation, so a password containing a quote breaks it. Use `json.dumps`.
- **Behaviour to keep:** the internal HA URL defaults to the request's hostname plus HA's port (override `advanced_internal_base_ha_url_and_port`). `homeassistant.local` can fail to resolve inside a container (mDNS); consider `http://homeassistant:8123` as the default, but test on the box first since this is an access path.
- **Code shape:** needs a small route registry in `server.py`, the draft module contract (`start()`, `stop()`, `health()`) with one module failing never stopping the others, and a second HTTP server on the guest port. QR: `segno` (pure Python, SVG) is the likely dependency.
- **Cut-over:** guest options are stored per app slug, so re-enter them once. Remove the `cnorick/ha-auto-guest-login` repository from the App store when ours works, which also removes its error.
- **Rule Zero applies:** bump the version first, add the changelog entry.

  - **The Kiosk mode page and Camera Dashboard deploys** warn before saving a dashboard someone has open in edit mode ("Ann is editing it on the Kitchen tablet: their next save will undo yours"), and the open editor is told the dashboard changed under it.
  - **Entering edit mode** warns when someone else already has that dashboard in edit mode, or when it was saved elsewhere since this page loaded it. Outside edit mode this is already handled: with the integration's "reload dashboards when they change" helper on (`cm-refresh.js`), a page reloads itself on `lovelace_updated` instead of leaving HA's easily missed "Refresh?" toast. In edit mode it rightly doesn't reload (that would throw the edit away), so that is the gap: there the warning should say the dashboard changed elsewhere and that saving now overwrites the newer copy.
  - Finding edit mode: `cm-refresh.js` reads `div.edit-mode` in `hui-root`'s shadow DOM (the trap this guards against is why it exists); prefer `lovelace.editMode` on `hui-root`, or patch `setEditMode` as the view editor patch does, and say in the log when neither is found (HA's frontend changes).

- **FONA alerts when a wall tablet goes quiet:** a kiosk offline for longer than a set time texts the admins, as FONA works when the internet does not.
- **Callers and texts on the tablets:** a call or text from a known person shows on the wall tablets (the Tablet Layout banners, once built), via an HA event the integration raises.
- **Card dev loop reloads the test tablet:** `tools/dev_cards` asks a chosen kiosk to reload after each build, instead of a manual refresh.
